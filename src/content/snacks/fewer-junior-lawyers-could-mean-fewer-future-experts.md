Junior lawyers learn through work that AI may allow firms to automate. Andy raises the succession problem this creates. If firms need fewer people doing that work today, how will they develop the experienced lawyers they need when their current partners retire?

Andy worries about what happens when the partners retire. Their expertise came from years at the desk. If fewer juniors get that practice, fewer people may be ready to take over. Yeager agrees that the gap is unresolved. If firms take people in only at the middle tier or above, he asks how they can grow their own talent.

Andy sees more room to learn independently in design and marketing. People can experiment, win clients and eventually open their own shops without working inside the highly regulated setting of a law firm. He finds the route less clear in law and accounting. Yeager acknowledges that he hasn't worked through all these gaps, including how firms nurture newcomers who can't get in.
