A client calls their lawyer at 4 pm after signing an agreement without legal review or due diligence. The contract is already binding. Shawn Yeager uses this example to explain what clients are paying for. The document is already there, and they need someone they trust to help them work out what to do about it.

That trust includes the ability to stay steady. Yeager recalls a chief commercial officer at a financial institution who told him he hires lawyers for a "well-regulated nervous system." The phrase captures the composure Yeager sees as part of the service. Producing a document is only part of the job when a client needs help with a difficult decision.

Pete describes his own reason for hiring lawyers and accountants as risk mitigation. He pays for advice about a risk he faces, then decides whether he is comfortable accepting it. The lawyer or accountant helps him understand the exposure, while the decision remains his.
