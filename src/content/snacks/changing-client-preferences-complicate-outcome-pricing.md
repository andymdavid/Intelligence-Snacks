A client can write a design brief, see the finished design and realise they want something else. Pete sees this as a problem with selling services for an agreed outcome, because doing what the client asked may leave them wanting more work. Their preferences change as they see the result.

He sees the same problem in large consulting projects. A transformation programme starts with a high-level scope based on guesses and assumptions about the client’s business. Years of change requests can follow. Providers can set boundaries around an outcome, but then charge extra for requests outside them.

That helps explain the appeal of buying a team. Shawn Yeager recognises it from his experience as both a buyer and a seller. Three or four people on a project feel safer because the client understands what they’re buying and feels able to manage it. When requirements shift, they can move those people onto different tasks.
