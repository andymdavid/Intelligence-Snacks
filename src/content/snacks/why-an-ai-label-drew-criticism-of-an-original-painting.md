Andy recalled seeing someone post a painting online and claim they'd generated a flawless version of an artist's work with AI. Commenters said it was clearly AI and pointed to a particular brushstroke as evidence. Then the poster revealed it was an original painting. The brushstroke they'd criticised was the artist's own.

Andy suspected many of those commenters wouldn't know who ran the big AI labs, and might only know ChatGPT. His explanation was that AI had become an easy target for anger about a wider economy where powerful companies could take other people's work and threaten their jobs. You wouldn't need to follow the companies closely to hear the promises about replacing workers. The same technology being sold as useful could start to feel like a threat before you'd tried it yourself.

He thought that might also explain some of the boos when speakers mentioned AI at graduation ceremonies. If you're leaving university and struggling to find a first job, predictions that AI will take entry-level work away arrive at a particularly bad time. You've spent years preparing to enter a profession, and now you're hearing that employers may need fewer beginners. A speaker praising AI's capabilities may be talking about exactly the work you'd hoped someone would pay you to do.
