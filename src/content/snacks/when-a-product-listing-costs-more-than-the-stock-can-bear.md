Conventional ecommerce works best when the effort behind a product page can be spread across many sales. For a short run or a one-off item, studio photography, copy and catalogue preparation can consume too much of the value of the stock. Some goods remain difficult to sell online not because buyers are absent, but because each possible sale carries an unusually high listing cost.

This is an opportunity Rod is focusing on with Shosho. A retailer can present fast-fashion pieces, end-of-line stock, collectibles and one-of-a-kind goods in sequence, clearing items without first creating a separate polished page for each one. The broadcast also turns the sales process into entertainment, giving viewers a reason to stay as new products appear.

This format is especially useful when inventory is thin, irregular or quick to date. A seller can announce one event, gather an audience and move through stock item by item, making a shared presentation do the work that many separate listings would otherwise require. Live commerce therefore opens an online route for merchandise whose low quantity or unique attributes make a conventional catalogue uneconomical.
