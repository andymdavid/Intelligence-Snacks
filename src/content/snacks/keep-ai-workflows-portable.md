Pete worries about a business defining all of its work inside one provider's agent tools. If it puts enough workflows into Claude Cowork, he says, it risks losing control when the provider changes the product or turns it off. A business owner who has spent years building a company needs a way to keep work running when a particular service becomes unavailable or stops doing the job well.

In Wingman, Pete puts the definition of the work in a layer above the agents, rather than relying on their internal hooks and skills libraries. He says this lets him move between agents while the workflows continue to operate broadly the same way. The results still vary. Returning the required JSON is one of the differences he notices, so changing an agent does not mean every response will be identical.

He already uses Maple through a proxy for some work he wants to keep private, alongside other coding agents. Mark describes a fuller Maple builder CLI as a later stage of its plans, and Pete wants to test that in Wingman when it arrives. Keeping the work outside the agent gives him somewhere to bring that future tool in without first making the business depend on it.
