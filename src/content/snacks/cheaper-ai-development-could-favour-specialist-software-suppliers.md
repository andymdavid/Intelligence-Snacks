Buy software from someone else and you don’t have to spend your day building and maintaining it. Pete expects that reason to keep buying to survive cheaper AI development. He’s less convinced that large software providers will keep their advantage. As it gets cheaper to have developers experiment and make changes, assembling a large team may offer less protection from competitors.

Those large teams can also end up far from their users. A provider serving many customers can struggle to understand one group’s problems, let alone respond to them. Pete expects a supplier working just for that group to produce better software faster and more cheaply. It doesn’t have to accommodate everyone else.

Developer-tool makers may be an exception. They use the tools they build and experience the problems inside their own organisations, so Pete thinks they may have reason to keep their current shape. For suppliers without that closeness to users, cheaper development leaves a practical question about how narrowly they can focus on a group whose problems they understand.
