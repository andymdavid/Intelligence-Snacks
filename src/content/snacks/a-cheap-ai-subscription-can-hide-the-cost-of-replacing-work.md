With a personal AI subscription, Pete finds he can get several people's work done for what feels like almost nothing. He regards those subscriptions as heavily subsidised. If you're planning to replace work across a business, that monthly fee doesn't tell you what it would cost to run AI for the whole organisation.

Pete recalls an OpenAI usage screenshot from a developer's experiment that he remembers as $1.5 million or $1.8 million in tokens in a month. The aim was to push AI as far as it could go. To use it to replace people's work at that spending rate, you'd need to save enough on the work to cover the token bill, and keep covering it every month.

Running AI across whole organisations also means finding GPUs and electricity for the work. Pete questioned whether there are enough GPUs in the world to take on all the tasks being promised. Compute and energy are constrained, so those predictions depend on how much work the available supply can actually support.
