Planning often crosses two kinds of work. Tasks and records benefit from a defined structure, while early ideas need the freedom of a whiteboard. A shared canvas can keep both visible without forcing them into the same structure, letting each tool retain the form best suited to its job.

This works like federated search, where one request goes to several sources and brings their results together instead of forcing everything into one index. A control room follows the same principle. Its screens appear integrated from the operator's seat even though separate systems sit behind them. On a planning canvas, placing a whiteboard beside a live task list creates a similar connection without requiring the tools themselves to be integrated.

The whiteboard can hold the evolving shape of a plan while the adjacent list shows the tasks associated with it. Someone can sketch possibilities, turn settled ideas into tasks and update those tasks without repeatedly switching between disconnected views. The canvas brings these tools together in one workspace, preserving both the freedom of visual thinking and the precision of structured records.
