Andy argues that Anthropic’s bleakest AI warnings may serve two commercial goals at once. In the enterprise market, an existential threat can jolt slow-moving organisations into action and open the door to the company’s engineers. In government, the same sense of urgency can accelerate demands for regulation.

Once AI is framed as a threat that could erase jobs, policymakers need expertise to understand and contain it. The labs sounding the alarm are then well placed to provide that expertise and help draft the response. Their warnings don’t just describe the stakes. They can also shape which companies governments treat as credible partners.

That position could let established labs influence the details in their own favour. Andy’s concern is that Anthropic and OpenAI might help create demanding rules, then secure exemptions that protect their own operations while making it harder for smaller competitors to enter the market. The conflict is between the public message and the commercial incentive. A company can warn that AI threatens everyone while benefiting from the regulatory urgency that warning creates.
