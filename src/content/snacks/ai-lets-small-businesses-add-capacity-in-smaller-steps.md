You're running out of hours. That doesn't mean you can afford another employee. Pete describes how each hire can add $100,000 or $200,000 a year in total costs, so revenue has to stretch far enough to cover the whole commitment. Needing help with some of the work isn't enough.

With AI, Pete argues, you can buy capability in $100 or $200 chunks rather than in units the size of a human job. That makes smaller changes possible. An owner can introduce a process or automate part of the workload as the business grows, instead of waiting until there's enough revenue to support another role. His illustrative comparison is perhaps $5,000 over a year for those improvements versus $100,000 for an employee.

The salary is only part of that commitment. Dr Mitch, who advises Australian small businesses, points to the responsibility owners feel for the people they hire. They know their employees' families. If revenue falls short, laying someone off means telling a person they care about that the income their household relies on is stopping. For Mitch, avoiding an unnecessary hire can reduce both the financial risk to the business and the responsibility of taking on another person's livelihood.
