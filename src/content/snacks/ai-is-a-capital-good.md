AI may look like a consumer product because people encounter it through models, apps and chat interfaces. Its greater power, however, is as a capital good that accelerates what people can already do. Someone with a sound idea or a strong business can use that capability to develop it faster, rather than treating access to AI as the finished source of value.

This distinction matters more as intelligence becomes broadly available. If everyone can access similar capabilities, the advantage does not necessarily remain with the model or the app that provides them. The same intelligence can be applied to different niches, geographies and needs, producing solutions shaped by the particular circumstances in which it is used.

The resulting value can therefore spread outwards instead of accumulating only at the access point. AI helps create the product, but it is not necessarily the product. The finished offering is the specific solution built around a concrete demand, closer to the people experiencing it. The goal is to become the person who uses AI to create value, not to mistake the AI itself for the value.
