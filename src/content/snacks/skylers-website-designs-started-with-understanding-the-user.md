Skyler's design work for the Satoshi Nakamoto Institute had fancy serif fonts and the feel of custom letterhead. Pete, who'd worked with Skyler on several design projects, thought it suited the people behind the institute. Skyler's work for Maple looked different, and Pete knew from working with him how much reasoning could go into those choices.

In their work together, that reasoning started with the problem they were trying to solve. They talked about who the users were and what they experienced day to day, then how to get closer to that experience. That might mean getting users to describe it or being there themselves to understand it.

If you're judging how easily AI could do that job, the finished website only gives you part of it. You can ask for a layout with fancy serif fonts and get something to look at. But that request already includes a choice about what would suit the client. Automating the layout can still leave you with the work Pete and Skyler were doing together, finding out what the design needs to solve.
