A senior lawyer at a 1,500-person US firm is weighing whether to hire a first-year associate at US$300,000 a year or a systems engineer. Shawn Yeager recounts the conversation from an event where the lawyer interviewed him. The lawyer described professional services, and law in particular, as “white collar manufacturing”. What lawyers ultimately sell, he said, is trust.

The engineer would document processes and workflows and build structures to support them. With a background in economics and engineering, the lawyer saw more value in that work than in adding another associate, though he had yet to make the hiring decision.

Yeager helps professional services firms rethink what they sell as AI compresses billable hours. He describes clients arriving with 80 per cent of the work already done and asking for a judgement call on the final deliverable. The partner still supplies judgement, while in Yeager’s view firms have fewer hours to sell.
