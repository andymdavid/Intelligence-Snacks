An agent with private data, access to incoming internet information and the ability to send information out combines what Mark calls the lethal trifecta. He worries that it could email someone unexpectedly or send personal documents elsewhere. Those abilities are useful individually, but putting them together leaves him concerned about how much control a user has over the agent's actions.

Maple plans to remove the outbound part to start. The agent would be able to receive online information and work with private data, Mark says, while its ability to write to the internet would be held back. That means limiting the work it can do for someone. His earlier description of the product starts with a vault of information and personal insights, with connections to email, calendars and documents coming later.

When Maple adds the ability to create artefacts and write online, Mark wants sandboxing around those processes and a final human approval step. The user would review an action before it goes out. He hopes the agent will eventually be able to make some decisions and communicate with the outside world on its own, but describes human review as the approach he wants to take first.
