Andy used the design firm Pentagram to imagine how a law firm might work with fewer juniors. Its senior partners have reputations built on their own bodies of work. If lawyers moved towards guaranteeing outcomes for clients, a senior partner taking that risk might prefer to do the work themselves rather than rely on a junior's interpretation. The firm could become much flatter, with experienced partners doing more of the work directly.

Those partners had decades to learn. They built up experience task by task until they could judge what they were taking on and feel confident accepting the risk. Streamlining junior work could remove some of that practice for the next generation, leaving fewer chances to work towards the responsibility a senior partner carries.

Finding that practice elsewhere isn't equally easy in every profession. A young designer can get the tools and start designing independently. A junior lawyer generally needs to work inside a firm, where there are restrictions on practising alone. Pete suggested apprenticeships might return, giving juniors a route to learn alongside the partners who still do the work.
