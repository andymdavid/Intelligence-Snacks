Lincoln wondered if people might one day connect their AI agents to services without using the services’ interfaces. Pete was sceptical. He said delegating through chat can leave you without the knowledge and context of what’s happening, while the chat history itself offers little structure. He has built interfaces to follow the work in a way that makes sense to him.

Lincoln tried building an interface too. He called it Harness and put it over the terminal he uses alongside his Obsidian notes and AI tools, with an eye to potentially offering it to consultants on his platform. But he found himself going back to the simple black-and-white terminal, whose output he likened to the Matrix.

Pete’s guess is that interfaces will remain, but serve smaller groups with more specific needs. Andy put the emphasis on the experience. An interface’s appearance matters less if the experience doesn’t work. For Lincoln, the terminal he kept returning to was more appealing than the interface he had built over it.
