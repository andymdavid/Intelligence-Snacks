Pete is willing to call AI intelligent. A definition based on learning, understanding, reasoning and applying knowledge to new situations makes him reconsider what qualifies as intelligence. AI does seem able to do those things. What troubles him is the wanting. It can work on a problem, but he doesn't see it expressing a need until a person supplies one.

He imagines a person sitting in a room, doing nothing until someone comes in and gives them a task. Even if they could do the job, that would seem strange. The missing desire is what bothers him about the comparison. It's also why AI feels to him like an extension of human ability rather than another being with concerns of its own.

Andy adds a complication. Ask an agent to build an app and it can make decisions you never spelled out, choosing how to get the work done. Those are still decisions. He wonders how much that freedom might expand, but he still thinks a person has to set the direction. The agent can decide how to build the app; someone has to decide why it's worth building.
