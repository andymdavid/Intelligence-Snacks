Jarrad spent two to three hours planning a capacity-management feature, then let agents build it over roughly a week. They kept asking him to choose between options, but he found himself saying yes to their recommendations. Halfway through, he changed the instruction, telling them to consult other models, reach consensus and proceed.

He also asked the agents to summarize their decisions for him to review after the build. The result still needed testing and small tweaks, but he was satisfied. In his wider workflow, he uses an orchestrator and frontier models to weigh choices, including how different user roles should interact with data.

Jarrad stays more involved in defining the experience, talking through product plans and visual flows with an agent. That judgment matters to him. On a separate encryption feature, the models made the experience too difficult, so he scaled their approach back. He also finds it easier to give models room to work when the codebase already has an established architecture.
