Andy wanted a business development app where he could focus on finding leads with Lara, his AI agent. He already had a lead-finding pipeline to build around. A conventional CRM wasn't what he wanted, but he couldn't say exactly what the app should look like either. So he gave Lara a broad account of his goals and some visual cues, with chat and other views she could create as needed, and let her build.

About half an hour later, he had an interface to try. It looked quite nice. But as he used it, he found himself looking at what was essentially the raw data model, far removed from what he needed. He still had to interpret the information himself. That gave him something specific to work on with Lara. He needed a layer that made the data make sense to him.

Andy reckoned he could have spent ten hours planning and still been unsure what he wanted. Instead, he had an app on screen and could pick apart what was wrong with it. He hadn't worked out all the answers. He had, though, reached a clearer request for the next version. It should help him understand the leads, rather than just display the data behind them.
