You can object to JPMorgan and still use a small regional bank. Pete proposes approaching AI in the same way, keeping useful tools while backing smaller alternatives. His concern is a company trying to do everything, then using regulation to keep competitors out. Giving up the tools altogether would leave you with fewer ways to do things for yourself, while the company you're angry with would still have them.

Backing smaller AI projects doesn't mean they can immediately replace everything a large provider does. For now, they struggle to compete on running models and making them available to users. Getting models from big companies can still be useful. You can then choose the software and services you use with those models for the control they give you over your work, supporting an alternative without waiting for it to supply the whole system.

Wingman, the agent software Pete works on, is where he wants to pursue that approach. Possible commercial deployments were already being considered, with simpler ways to offer the software to more people. Choosing to use a project like that is one way to support its development. The hope is that more users will help these services improve, so there's somewhere useful to take your work even while the big companies continue to supply the models.
