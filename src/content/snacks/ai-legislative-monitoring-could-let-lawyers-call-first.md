A client calls or emails, the firm agrees a statement of work, and the project starts. Shawn Yeager proposes reversing that familiar sequence in legal services. AI agents could monitor new and proposed legislation in the background, giving the firm a reason to contact a client before the client asks for help.

He describes this as a move towards continuous compliance. A relevant legislative development would prompt the firm to make the first call, rather than wait for a new commission. The proposal gives ongoing monitoring a place in the service, alongside the projects that begin with a client request. Yeager presents it as a possible way of working, rather than a system already in use.

That also raises the question of what the firm promises to deliver. Later in the discussion, Yeager uses tax compliance to distinguish an ultimate goal from the smaller services that contribute to it. Keeping a client compliant all year is the goal. The individual pieces of work need a scope the firm can define and sell.
