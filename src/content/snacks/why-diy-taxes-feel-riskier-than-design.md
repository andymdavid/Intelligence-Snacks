Shawn Yeager thinks he could theoretically handle most of his own tax filing. He still happily pays for help. He describes a personal red line he will approach but will not cross, even when he believes he could do the work himself. Being able to attempt the task does not make him willing to accept the risk.

A new logo or web design is another matter. He is happy to give those a go because the stakes are different. Earlier in the discussion, he contrasts a marketing client disliking a piece of work with the possibility of fraud accusations over an incorrect Medicare claim. Mistakes in professional services can carry very different consequences.

His tax example gives that distinction a practical form. He expects to keep paying for help with work he thinks he could largely do himself. For firms considering how AI changes demand for their services, that is a reason to look beyond which tasks clients can now complete. A client’s willingness to risk being wrong also affects what they will buy.
