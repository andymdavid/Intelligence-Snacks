Consultants have talked about charging for outcomes instead of hours for years. Lincoln says his work has always focused on outcomes, but clients find it easier to understand a price based on the hours people work. Pete says clients can see an outcome-based fee as a way to charge more for something they don’t understand; they want to know how many people the job takes.

The hours on the bill are only part of the picture. Lincoln says a client is also buying the experience a consultant has accumulated over a lifetime. Consultants themselves can forget what they’ve learned, he says, and give too much of it away when pricing by the hour.

Lincoln sees a limit to charging for outcomes. It works until clients can reproduce them without the consultant. Some of his long-term clients have tried taking the work in-house, he says, then called him about five months later because they found it hard. He thinks clients will keep paying people who deliver outcomes. The harder part, in his view, will be convincing new clients they can deliver them.
