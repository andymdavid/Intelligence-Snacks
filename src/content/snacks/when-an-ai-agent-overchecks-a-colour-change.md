Pete doesn't mind a coding agent spending time checking its work when he's given it a big goal and left it running for a few hours. He isn't waiting on the next interaction. He suspects some of the improvement in agents comes from how they organise the work, looping back, questioning their own results and checking again.

But a colour change? He wants the agent to make the edit and send it back so he can judge it himself. Instead, he finds himself interrupting tests he hasn't asked for. In his recent experience with coding models, agents fire up simulators, read the screen and inspect the running app. The results are better, he says. They also take longer.

For those small changes, Pete wants the agent to ship the edit to his phone and let him say whether it's right. He's ready to look at the colour. The checks he welcomes during an unattended build now hold up the next exchange.
