Shawn Yeager encourages firms to own the instructions that organise their AI work, including workflows and scheduling. There's only so much they can buy off the shelf, he argues, before they outsource the capability of a whole business function. His recommendation is to build and own that coordination, then treat inference, the model’s processing, as a service they can swap in and out.

Yeager points to open standards and files that different tools can read to guide their output. The firm can keep those instructions when it changes suppliers. Even a choice of four or five providers would mean avoiding dependence on just one, he says. He also expects firms that have documented how they work to find it easier to put agents to use.

Pete offers a practical test. If Claude goes offline, a business should be able to move to Codex, Pi, Goose or local models without meaningfully disrupting its work. He sees the processes that define how work gets done as the core assets a business should retain. Yeager’s proposal to use text files across tools gives those instructions somewhere to live beyond a single supplier’s product.
