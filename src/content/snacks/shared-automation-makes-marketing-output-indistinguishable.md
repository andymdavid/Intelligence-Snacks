Automating marketing production can make content abundant without making it distinctive. If every business can run a similar pipeline and fill the same channels with comparable material, publishing more offers little advantage because audiences have no particular reason to notice one business over another. The challenge becomes finding original ways to reach customers and earn their attention.

That shift also changes what gives a message credibility. A full-page New York Times advertisement can still convey authority because of where it appears, while two word-for-word identical posts can be received very differently when one comes from someone who has spent years building an audience, earning trust and demonstrating their expertise. The words alone don't determine how people receive them.

Direct relationships become another valuable asset. Kevin Kelly's idea of one thousand true fans is that a committed audience can be valuable in its own right, rather than merely a stepping stone towards mass reach. As automated content becomes easier to produce, original work and established relationships may become more valuable, even as the growing volume of content makes them harder to discover.
