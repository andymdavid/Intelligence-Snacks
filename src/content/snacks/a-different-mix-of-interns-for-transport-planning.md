Before the pandemic, Andy ran an education technology company focused on work-integrated learning. For one summer internship programme, his team worked with a large engineering firm to build projects around work its transport planning team had scope to do in-house.

They compared two groups. One was made up of third- and fourth-year planning engineers; the other drew on different disciplines. The mixed team included a biology student, a teacher and an engineering PhD student. Andy said the firm got substantially better outcomes from that team and hired from the group.

The experience challenged an assumption Andy had encountered in graduate programmes, where young people were treated as blank slates who needed training from scratch. For him, it suggested employers could rethink both whom they hire and what they ask them to do.
