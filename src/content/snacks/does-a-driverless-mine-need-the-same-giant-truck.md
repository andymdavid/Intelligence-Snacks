A mining truck has tyres taller than a person, yet one driver moves the whole load. Pete describes its size as a compromise between employing fewer drivers and managing the maintenance demands and risks of a bigger vehicle. Take the driver out, and that compromise changes. Why keep the same dimensions?

Pete suggests a fleet of smaller automated vehicles that are easy to replace. Andy goes further, suggesting that perhaps a conveyor belt could carry the ore straight to a train. Neither is a settled answer. Both start by asking how to move the ore, rather than how to automate the giant truck.

At an existing mine, keeping the truck can make sense. Andy points out that the infrastructure is already there. A new mine leaves more room to rethink the design, but someone still has to fund it and know how to run it. Lenders may only back a method they know, and operators have experience with the equipment they already use. A mine could lose the driver and still have good reasons to keep the truck.
