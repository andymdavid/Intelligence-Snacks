Mark does not see himself replacing many jobs with AI in his day-to-day work at Maple. He is doing more as a founder and holding off hiring for longer, which stretches the company's money. Marketing is a limit. He would like an AI marketing person, but has no confidence that he could simply hand the work over. He would still need to prompt it, guide it and review what came back.

Andy sees room for small teams to test ideas that once needed more capital. Mark knows people who have given themselves some runway to run their own small incubators. Someone could build a launch website, a prototype and a mailing list for 10 or 20 products, he says, then put more effort into the one that gains traction. The early work becomes a way to find a business worth pursuing before building a larger team around it.

Pete expects himself and agents to produce more software than he might once have expected from five engineers. That changes how much hiring he can imagine doing, but he agrees with Mark about oversight. For the podcast's marketing, he doubts AI could do the job well on its own yet. For the show, he says, a person still has to oversee the work, and producing AI slop to build an audience would not fit its principles.
