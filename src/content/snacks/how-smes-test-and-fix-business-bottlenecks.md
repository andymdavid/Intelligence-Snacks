Kane describes Regional Prosperity’s approach as firing a bullet before reaching for the bazooka. A micro test shows whether a client responds or a process works before the firm commits more time and money. Small businesses can run that learning loop quickly because they do not have to lodge a request, wait for a board meeting and seek permission from several layers of management.

That speed only matters when the experiment targets the right problem. Regional Prosperity’s constraint had long been technology. The firm was attracting work but could not process it efficiently, so Kane concentrated investment on operational capacity. Promoting the brand at the same time would have generated more enquiries for a system that was already struggling with the workload.

Once the technology bottleneck eased and the team could move work through the business, brand became the next focus. The sequence is the discipline. Identify the constraint, run a small test, learn from the response and invest further only when the evidence supports it. Regional Prosperity could then create more demand without overwhelming operations, while retaining the freedom to test the next improvement before making a larger commitment.
