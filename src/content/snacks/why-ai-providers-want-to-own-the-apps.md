A better model used to be enough to make some users switch subscriptions. Andy describes people cancelling Claude for OpenAI, then moving back again a few months later. As models become more interchangeable, he argues, providers need a way to make customers less likely to leave.

His answer is the application layer. If providers build the tools people work in and gather data about how that work gets done, Andy suggests, they can raise the cost of switching even when a rival releases a better model. He points to Microsoft’s hold on enterprise customers as an example of that kind of stickiness.

Jarrad dislikes seeing providers compete with businesses building on their models, and worries they will spread themselves too thin. He thinks great products need dedicated teams that understand the problem. Pete sees room for smaller applications built with more care, while Andy sees an opportunity in specialised needs that broad tools may miss.
