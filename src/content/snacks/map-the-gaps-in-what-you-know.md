A personal knowledge graph shows the ideas someone already understands and the links between them. A broader map of the same subject can expose nearby concepts that are missing from that personal view. The comparison gives learning a direction by showing not only what is unknown, but which gaps sit close enough to existing knowledge to be useful next steps.

This is the advantage a teacher or mentor often provides. Their more developed understanding lets them see beyond a learner's current boundary and identify a concept whose relevance is not yet visible from inside the smaller map. An agent could play a similar role by comparing what a person has consumed with a wider view of the subject, then guiding them towards the next reachable area.

The agent would not need a complete public graph of the field. It could explore only one or two connections beyond the learner's current position, looking for adjacent topics and then finding relevant people on Twitter, podcasts or other sources that develop them. The task is not to represent everything. It is to stay a couple of pages ahead and map the nearest useful territory.
